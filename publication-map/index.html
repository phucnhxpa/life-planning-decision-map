Europe does make the global cut—but unevenly.

The UK places Oxford and Cambridge inside the global university top 25. Switzerland narrowly misses: ETH is #27 and EPFL #33. No French education-coded institution reaches the top 25 in this extraction; the highest is Supélec at #65. This is publication-time affiliation, not proof of undergraduate causality.

Interpret carefully

UK #3: 5.96% of pooled country fractional credit.

Switzerland #9: 1.84%.

France #12: 1.33%.

Country attribution is less vulnerable than institution ranking to French affiliation fragmentation, but it still records where authors were affiliated when publishing—not where they studied as undergraduates.

These three venues are an ML case study. Physics and neuroscience use different publication systems.

What this means for Phuc

Do not read the squares as undergraduate treatment effects. They show where papers were produced. They do not tell us the probability that the same student would publish after choosing one university over another.

The correct university test remains: verified access to active mentors, sustained research ownership, technical depth, strong letters, external placements, affordability and legal/timing fit. Oxford or Cambridge can be a real advantage when those mechanisms are personally accessible. ETH and EPFL are globally competitive in this slice despite missing the arbitrary top-25 boundary. France needs a deliberate substitution strategy: excellent grades, external labs, internships, co-supervision and owned outputs.
