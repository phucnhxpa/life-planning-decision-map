What this means for Phuc

Do not read the squares as undergraduate treatment effects. They show where papers were produced. They do not tell us the probability that the same student would publish after choosing one university over another.

The correct university test remains: verified access to active mentors, sustained research ownership, technical depth, strong letters, external placements, affordability and legal/timing fit. Oxford or Cambridge can be a real advantage when those mechanisms are personally accessible. ETH and EPFL are globally competitive in this slice despite missing the arbitrary top-25 boundary. France needs a deliberate substitution strategy: excellent grades, external labs, internships, co-supervision and owned outputs.

Field 2 — Theoretical physics

Published benchmark first · Nature Index 2025 Research Leaders · 2024 output

I found an existing independent benchmark and use it before the custom analysis: the Nature Index academic table for physical sciences. It places Cambridge #11, ETH #14, Oxford #16, Paris-Saclay #42, Sorbonne #48 and EPFL #50. Its country table places the UK #4, France #6 and Switzerland #13; by its fractional Share metric, the UK is 1.72× France.

This is higher-authority published data with transparent institutional normalization, but it covers the broader physical sciences—not theoretical physics alone. Nature Index tracks a researcher-selected set of high-quality journals, and its Share metric fractionally allocates each paper across authors' affiliations. How the supplement was defined

This is a reproducible secondary analysis of the existing OpenAlex database, not a previously published study. I kept it only because the Nature Index benchmark is too broad to isolate theoretical physics. The dataset includes OpenAlex journal articles tagged with the Theoretical physics concept, then selects exactly the top 10% by citation count separately within each year.

Minimum citation counts in the selected sets were 50 for 2019, 46 for 2020 and 37 for 2021. Citation impact is observational, field-tagging can be noisy, and institution affiliations still do not identify undergraduate causality.
