How to read the academic routes: Option D is the only route with the full French sequence: learn French through 2028 → complete the entire three-year French undergraduate degree in 2028–31 → complete the default two-year French physics master in 2031–33. Routes containing Cambridge undergraduate years instead proceed to a UK master lasting one or two years before the PhD. Do not attach the French master to a partial-Cambridge route. Five years is only a French-naturalisation eligibility-planning threshold, not guaranteed approval. The robust legal sequence remains genuinely France-based → apply in 2029 → remain France-centred during processing → signed decree → verify status → relocate; if the decree is unexpectedly still pending, remain France-centred rather than relocating. The long-term resident–EU card is an optional fallback, not a step before citizenship. The 0–3-year preparation range remains only on the 2027 non-Cambridge row. Option B’s Year-2 transfer still requires written confirmation from the exact Cambridge course and College. UK: Student years count toward ten-year Long Residence, but not the separate Global Talent or Skilled Worker settlement clocks. US: a PhD does not grant a Green Card. Every Green Card date is a planning assumption requiring a separately successful immigrant route—such as qualifying employer sponsorship, EB-1 or EB-2 NIW—and visa availability. The ordinary five-year naturalisation clock begins only with lawful permanent residence, not F-1, OPT, H-1B or O-1. Here, “bridge” means the temporary post-PhD period in a valid nonimmigrant work status while an independent Green Card route is pursued; “LPR” means lawful permanent resident—the legal status shown by a Green Card; citizenship comes only after N-400 approval and the oath.
